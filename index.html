Earth Jukebox NASA EIC Challenge

Hear what NASA pictures of Earth show: colors and brightness become sound.

How to use

  1. Choose one of the three datasets.
  2. Upload a video, or as many pictures as you like (JPG, PNG or PDF).
  3. Press Play. The yellow line reads the picture and turns its colors and brightness into sound.

World border alignment editor

Edit mode lets you change the position and size of the country borders. Drag the middle of the map to move it; drag a handle on the left, right, top or bottom edge to change only that edge. Drag a corner handle to enlarge or shrink the borders in proportion. These settings belong to the selected dataset only.

Extra border frames: each border frame is one complete set of country borders (like the first one). Use them when a picture holds more than one world map, or a map of a different size. A pixel is read with the newest border frame that covers it, and the results of all border frames are added together.

33D coil of the analysis

Each ring is one frame. Its 32 points are the readings of the scan line (left to right). Farther from the center = higher value; the color follows the dataset: temperature for the ocean, brightness for night lights, dot count for lightning. Drag to turn it, click a ring to select that frame.

About

You upload a video (analyzed live while it plays) or any number of JPG/PNG images or PDF pages for one of the three datasets. Use flat world-map frames that match the NASA reference; globe, orbit, cropped regional and inset-heavy frames are not suitable for geographic attribution. Each frame is split into 32 columns and read pixel by pixel. Notes are snapped to a pentatonic scale so the result stays musical. The frames are real NASA SVS / EIC visualizations: Black Marble night lights, the ECCO2 ocean model and the ISS Lightning Imaging Sensor. To add more, see docs/ADD_DATA.md. Credit: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio.

AI disclosure

  • Claude (Anthropic), an AI assistant, helped the team with ideas, with code (including the country detection, the upload reading, the voice and accessibility features) and with drafting the presentation slides. The team chose the project, directed the work, reviewed, tested and is responsible for the result.
  • The “AI” inside the web app is a small k-nearest-neighbour classifier that runs in your browser. It learns from the NASA sample frames shipped with the project and only tells you which of the three datasets a picture looks like. It is not a generative model and calls no external AI service.
  • Conclusions shown by the app are generated by fixed rules from the numbers measured in the pictures; they are image-based estimates, not official NASA measurements.
